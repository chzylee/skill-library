# Research grounding — why content-starter's scaffolds work

This file grounds the pieces content-starter **owns**: the outline scaffolds (the funnel
spine, the platform shapes) and the **Agency Law** (the sovereignty/enforcement layer).
Each traces to a researched mechanism, so the skill behaves as a *reviewed advisor* about
*structure* — not generic tips.

> **Scope note (post-refactor 2026-07-15):** the *stance* — the data-driven bias, the four
> modes and their biases, and grounding-via-runtime-research — moved to the **Content
> strategist** persona and is grounded there (that persona grounds via live research, not
> this static file). What remains here is only what justifies content-starter's own
> layers: the shapes and the enforcement rule. The coach does **not** cite these at
> runtime; the research shapes its judgment, not its output.

Honesty about evidence strength is part of the point: the claim is "each shape traces to a
source you can check," **not** "every shape is proven." Strength is flagged per row.

| Element content-starter owns | Mechanism it applies | Primary source | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| The default spine (Hook → Draw-in → Hold → Ender) | Attention → interest → desire → action progression | E. St. Elmo Lewis, AIDA (1898); codified by Strong (1925) | Historical / foundational |
| Hook beat — win the first seconds | Most short-form drop-off is in the first ~3s; leading with the payoff (not branding) lifts retention | *Decoding the Hook* (arXiv 2026); platform retention data | Practitioner + emerging CS |
| Draw-in / Hold — open one curiosity gap | Curiosity is felt deprivation from a perceived knowledge gap; an opened gap drives resolution-seeking | Loewenstein (1994), *Psychological Bulletin* 116:75–98; Zeigarnik (1927) | Peer-reviewed (Zeigarnik memory effect replicates weakly) |
| Hold — concrete example over abstract claim | Concrete language is recalled better, judged more truthful, shared and bought more | *J. Consumer Research* 47:787 (2020); concrete-language sharing studies (2025) | Peer-reviewed |
| Story-shaped Hold — build an absorbing arc | "Transportation" (absorption into narrative) increases story-consistent beliefs and favorable evaluations | Green & Brock (2000), *JPSP* 79:701–721 | Peer-reviewed |
| Native-format fit (platform scaffolds vary the shape) | Shaping to where content lives outperforms a portable "good" template | practitioner platform norms; consistent with the persona's "native-format fit" pattern | Practitioner heuristic |
| The Agency Law (bias only in the open, user-chosen, never past values) | Opinionated AI writing assistants shift what users write *and think*; AI can steer people from their values | Jakesch et al. (2023), CHI; Nishal, Lee et al. (2026), CHI | Peer-reviewed |

## Notes that change how to apply a shape
- **Hook (first seconds):** the strongest numbers are practitioner/platform data plus
  nascent computational work — treat "first 3 seconds" as a well-supported heuristic, not
  a law. Front-load the concrete payoff; don't over-claim.
- **Open loop:** the *curiosity-gap* mechanism (Loewenstein) is the sturdy citation; the
  Zeigarnik *memory* effect replicates weakly, so use **one** open loop and don't rely on
  it as the whole retention strategy.
- **Shapes flex, they aren't laws:** the scaffolds are research-informed *defaults*. The
  persona's pattern is "shape is derived from research, not a fixed template," so a scoped
  research pass can and should revise a scaffold when current patterns say otherwise.
- **Agency Law:** Jakesch et al. is direct experimental evidence (1,506 participants) that
  a persistent tool bias reshapes the user; that's the empirical reason bias must be
  user-chosen, named when it acts, and never allowed to override stated values. This is
  why enforcement sits in the consumer (where the user is), not in the persona.
